Training

The push pull legs split, written out

Push pull legs groups your training by movement: pushing muscles one day, pulling muscles the next, legs the third. It is simple to follow, easy to fit into any week and scales from three days to six. Here are both versions with every exercise, set and rep.

Published 26 November 2026 · 5 min read

How the push pull legs split works

  • Push: chest, shoulders and triceps. Presses, raises, pushdowns.
  • Pull: back, rear shoulders and biceps. Rows, pull-ups, curls.
  • Legs: quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves, plus core work.

Because the muscles worked on push days rest on pull and leg days, you can train often without the same muscle being sore every session. That is why push pull legs is one of the most popular splits for building muscle.

3-day or 6-day: which one

3 days a week (each muscle once a week) is the right choice if you are busy, a beginner, or also doing a sport. It works, but each session needs to be complete because you will not see that muscle again for a week.

6 days a week (each muscle twice a week) gives more weekly volume and usually faster muscle gain for people who recover well. It is a lot of gym time. Only choose it if you can sleep 7-9 hours and eat enough, and if you have trained consistently for at least six months.

If you are not sure, start with three days. You can move to six later. An in-between option is to rotate push, pull and legs across four days a week, so the order rolls on regardless of the weekday.

The 3-day push pull legs plan

Schedule: Monday push, Wednesday pull, Friday legs. Rest or walk on the other days.

Push

  • Bench press: 4 sets of 6-8
  • Overhead press: 3 sets of 8-10
  • Incline dumbbell press: 3 sets of 8-12
  • Lateral raise: 3 sets of 12-15
  • Triceps pushdown: 3 sets of 10-12
  • Overhead triceps extension: 2 sets of 10-12

Pull

  • Pull-ups or lat pulldown: 4 sets of 6-10
  • Barbell or chest-supported row: 3 sets of 8-10
  • Seated cable row: 3 sets of 10-12
  • Face pull: 3 sets of 12-15
  • Barbell or dumbbell curl: 3 sets of 8-12
  • Hammer curl: 2 sets of 10-12

Legs

  • Squat: 4 sets of 5-8
  • Romanian deadlift: 3 sets of 8-10
  • Leg press or split squat: 3 sets of 10-12
  • Leg curl: 3 sets of 10-12
  • Standing calf raise: 3 sets of 10-15
  • Hanging knee raise or plank: 3 sets

The 6-day push pull legs plan

Schedule: push, pull, legs, push, pull, legs, rest. Run the 3-day sessions above as the A days. On the B days, change the main lifts so the week is balanced and joints get some variety:

  • Push B: incline barbell press 4 x 6-8, seated dumbbell press 3 x 8-12, machine or dip chest press 3 x 10-12, cable lateral raise 3 x 12-15, skull crusher 3 x 10-12.
  • Pull B: deadlift or trap-bar deadlift 3 x 4-6, one-arm dumbbell row 3 x 8-12, wide-grip pulldown 3 x 10-12, rear delt fly 3 x 12-15, incline curl 3 x 10-12.
  • Legs B: front squat or hack squat 3 x 6-10, hip thrust 3 x 8-12, walking lunge 2 x 10 per leg, leg extension 3 x 12-15, seated calf raise 3 x 12-15.

Because you train each muscle twice, keep most sets 1-3 reps short of failure. Going to failure on every set six days a week is the fastest way to burn out.

Before each session

  • Check last session's weights and reps
  • Warm up with 2-3 lighter sets on the first lift
  • Rest 2-3 minutes on big lifts, 60-90 seconds on small ones
  • Log every working set
  • Finish within about 75 minutes

How to progress on push pull legs

Use double progression on every exercise. When you hit the top of the rep range on all working sets, add the smallest weight jump next time and start at the bottom of the range again. It is explained step by step, with numbers, in our guide to progressive overload.

Keep the same exercises for at least 6-8 weeks so you can see progress. Every 6-10 weeks, take a lighter week with about half the sets at the same weights.

Food, sleep and recovery

Aim for 7-9 hours of sleep and protein around 1.6 g per kg of body weight a day. To build muscle, eat at maintenance or a little above; to lose fat, eat in a moderate deficit and expect slower strength gains. Walk on rest days. If you have an injury or medical condition, check with a doctor or physio before starting.

Push pull legs vs upper/lower

Both work. Upper/lower is usually four days and suits people who want each muscle twice a week without six sessions; our Winter Arc workout plan is a good example. Push pull legs is easier to scale up or down. The best split is the one you will actually do every week, and that part is about showing up, which our guide on how to be disciplined covers.

FAQ

Is push pull legs good for beginners?

Yes, especially the 3-day version. It is simple to follow and teaches the main lifts. Move to six days only after several months of consistent training.

Is a 3-day push pull legs split enough to build muscle?

It can be, if you train hard, progress steadily and eat enough protein. Six days usually builds muscle faster for people who can recover from it.

What is the best order for push pull legs?

Push, pull, legs is the most common because the upper-body days are separated by a different muscle group. Any order works as long as you keep it consistent.

How long should a push pull legs workout take?

Around 60-75 minutes including warm-up and rest. If it takes much longer, cut a set from the smaller exercises.
