The Winter Arc diet: what to eat and how much

There is no official Winter Arc diet, and you do not need one. You need a direction, a calorie target, a protein target and a handful of meals you can repeat when it is cold, dark and you do not feel like cooking. Here is how to set that up in one evening.

Published 23 November 2026 · 6 min read

Step 1: choose one direction

The most common mistake in a Winter Arc is trying to lose fat fast and gain muscle fast at the same time, then changing the plan every ten days. Pick one direction and hold it for at least eight weeks.

  • Cut if you carry more body fat than you want and your main goal is to look and feel leaner by the end of the arc.
  • Lean bulk if you are already fairly lean, you train hard and your lifts have stopped moving.
  • Maintain if you are new to lifting, coming back after a long break. Beginners can often build muscle and lose some fat near maintenance, as long as protein and training are in place.

Step 2: set your calories

Maintenance calories are what keep your weight stable. A rough starting point for a moderately active adult is body weight in pounds times 14 to 16. Use the low end if you sit most of the day and the high end if you are on your feet. Then adjust for your direction:

  • Cut: 300 to 500 calories below maintenance. Aim to lose about 0.5 to 1 percent of your body weight per week.
  • Lean bulk: 150 to 300 calories above maintenance. Aim for roughly 0.25 percent of body weight per week.
  • Maintain: stay within about 100 calories of the number, averaged across the week.

Three worked examples

  • 150 lb: maintenance around 2,250. Cut near 1,850, lean bulk near 2,450, protein about 105 g.
  • 180 lb: maintenance around 2,700. Cut near 2,300, lean bulk near 2,900, protein about 125 g.
  • 210 lb: maintenance around 3,150. Cut near 2,750, lean bulk near 3,350, protein about 150 g.

Every formula is a guess. Your scale is the correction. Eat the number for two weeks, compare the weekly averages and move by 100 to 150 calories if the trend is wrong. Ignore single mornings: salt, carbs and a late dinner can move the scale by a few pounds without any change in body fat.

Step 3: hit protein first

If you only track one thing, track protein. It supports muscle while you diet and it keeps you full. A solid target is 1.6 g per kg of body weight, about 0.7 g per pound. Going somewhat higher during a cut is fine for healthy people. Split it over three or four meals.

Once protein is set, keep fat at a reasonable level (around 0.3 g per pound is a common minimum) and let carbs fill the rest. On a 2,300 calorie cut, that could look like 140 g of protein, 70 g of fat and 275 g of carbs.

Step 4: build meals you can repeat

A winter diet works when it is boring in the right places. Keep two breakfasts, two lunches and four dinners in rotation. All numbers below are estimates and depend on brand and portion:

  • Greek yogurt and oats: 1 cup of nonfat Greek yogurt, half a cup of oats and berries. Around 28 g of protein.
  • Eggs on toast: 3 eggs plus half a cup of cottage cheese on two slices of bread. Around 35 to 40 g.
  • Chicken and rice bowl: 6 oz of cooked chicken breast, a cup of rice and vegetables. Around 55 to 60 g.
  • Beef and bean chili: 5 oz of cooked lean beef, beans, tomatoes and onion. Around 45 to 50 g, and it freezes well.
  • Lentil soup with tofu: a cup of cooked lentils and half a block of firm tofu. Around 35 g.

A sample day near 2,300 calories

  • Breakfast: Greek yogurt, oats, banana, berries and a spoon of peanut butter. About 540 calories, 33 g protein.
  • Lunch: chicken and rice bowl with a tablespoon of olive oil. About 665 calories, 59 g protein.
  • Snack: an apple and an ounce of almonds. About 260 calories, 6 g protein.
  • Dinner: beef and bean chili with a little cheese. About 670 calories, 58 g protein.
  • Flexible: 160 calories for milk in your coffee or something sweet. Total: about 2,295 calories and 155 g of protein.

Winter Arc diet setup

  • I chose one direction: cut, maintain or lean bulk
  • My daily calorie target is __
  • My daily protein target is __ g
  • I have two breakfasts and four dinners I can repeat
  • I weigh myself in the morning and look at the weekly average
  • I know my plan for the next holiday meal

Step 5: plan for the holidays

Thanksgiving, work parties and late December land right in the middle of most arcs. One big dinner will not ruin eight weeks of work. Treating it as the end of the plan will. Use simple rules:

  1. Protein first. Turkey, ham, fish or beans go on the plate before the sides.
  2. Do not starve all day. Eat a normal breakfast with protein, or you arrive hungry and lose control.
  3. Pick your favorites. Have the two things you only get once a year and skip what you can buy any Tuesday.
  4. Train that morning or walk.
  5. Back to normal at the next meal. Not on Monday. Not in January.

If you are cutting out sugar, decide before you arrive, not at the table. The Winter Arc rules guide helps you choose a "don't" you can hold.

Winter Arc diet mistakes to avoid

  • Cutting too hard. A 1,000 calorie deficit in a season of heavy training usually ends in poor sleep, weak sessions and a binge.
  • Forgetting drinks and oils. Lattes, juice, alcohol and cooking oil are easy to miss and add up quickly.
  • Eating clean but not counting. Nuts, granola and avocado are good food with a lot of calories.
  • Logging only good days. The messy days are the ones that explain the scale.

FAQ

What should I eat during a Winter Arc?

Mostly simple meals built around a protein source, a carb you tolerate well and vegetables, at a calorie level that fits your goal. Repeating a few meals makes it far easier to stay on target.

How much protein do I need for a Winter Arc?

A good target for people who lift is around 1.6 g per kg of body weight, or 0.7 g per pound. For someone at 180 lb that is about 125 g a day.

How much weight can I lose in a Winter Arc?

A sensible pace is about 0.5 to 1 percent of body weight per week. For a 180 lb person that is roughly 1 to 2 lb a week, so 8 to 12 weeks can make a clear difference.

Do I have to count calories on a Winter Arc diet?

No, but counting for at least two weeks shows you what you really eat. After that, many people only track protein, body weight and a few repeat meals.
