Quit a habit

How to quit short videos and stay off them

You open the app for a minute and look up 50 minutes later. If that happens most days, cutting down rarely holds for long. This guide shows how to quit TikTok and other short-video feeds step by step, what to put in their place and how to handle a slip.

Published 23 November 2026 · 6 min read

Why "just use it less" rarely holds

Short-video feeds have no natural end. A show finishes, a chapter closes, a game has a final whistle. A feed always has one more clip, each is only seconds long, and now and then one is great. That mix makes it easy to start and hard to stop.

It also explains why a soft limit fails. "Less" has to be decided again every time you pick up the phone, usually when you are tired. "None for 30 days" is decided once.

Before you quit: 20 minutes of prep

  1. Check your real number. On iPhone, open Settings, then Screen Time, and look at your weekly average for each app. Write it down.
  2. Write your reason in one sentence. "I want my evenings back" is better than "it is bad for me".
  3. List your three trigger moments. For most people: in bed, on the toilet and in the gap after work or class.
  4. Choose the line. No short videos in any app, for 30 days. If you quit one feed and keep another, you have not quit, you have moved.
  5. Save what you need. Recipes, workouts, contacts. If you post videos, request a copy of your data first.

How to quit TikTok step by step

Step 1: remove the app and the back doors

Delete the app. Then log out in every browser on your phone and laptop and delete the saved password. Do the same for the other places where you watch short videos, or stop using the tab that holds them. The goal is at least two minutes of effort between the urge and the first clip.

Step 2: decide about the account

Deleting the app does not touch your account. At the time of writing, you can deactivate or delete a TikTok account in the app under Profile, Menu, Settings and privacy, Account. Deactivating hides your profile and you can come back by logging in. Deleting becomes permanent after a 30-day window, during which logging in restores the account. Menus change, so check the app's own help pages. If you are unsure, remove the app now and decide about the account on day 30.

Step 3: make the phone boring

  • Turn off all notifications that do not come from a real person.
  • Move the remaining fun apps off the first home screen.
  • Charge the phone outside the bedroom and buy a cheap alarm clock.
  • Turn off autoplay in the apps you keep.

Step 4: put something in the gap

If the app took 90 minutes a day, you now have more than ten hours a week to fill. Fill them on purpose, by trigger:

  • In bed: a paperback and a lamp. Ten pages count.
  • In the bathroom: leave the phone outside. Really.
  • After work or class: gym clothes on first, then a workout or a 20-minute walk.
  • Short waits: music, a podcast, or nothing at all. Boredom is allowed.
  • Stuck on a task: write the next small step on paper and do two minutes of it.
  • Lonely: call or meet one person.

Step 5: check in at your weak hour

Most slips happen in the late afternoon and evening. Set one daily check-in before that window and answer two questions: am I still clean today, and what will I do tonight? Your quit-day checklist

  • Weekly screen time written down
  • Reason written in one sentence
  • Short-video apps deleted
  • Logged out in all browsers
  • Notifications off, charger out of the bedroom
  • One replacement chosen for each trigger
  • One person told
  • End date written down: 30 days from today

What to expect in 30 days

Everyone is different, but this is a useful map:

  • Days 1 to 3: your thumb goes to the empty spot on the screen. Restless, a bit bored. Keep your hands busy.
  • Days 4 to 7: the urges come in clusters, mostly at your trigger times. Evenings feel long. Use them.
  • Days 8 to 14: it becomes less automatic. This is when you may tell yourself you are cured and can watch "just a few". Do not.
  • Days 15 to 21: new routines start to hold. Many people notice they fall asleep earlier simply because nothing keeps them up.
  • Days 22 to 30: the question changes from "can I do this" to "do I want it back".

An urge is not an order. It usually fades within minutes if you do not feed it. Put the phone down, stand up, drink a glass of water, walk to another room. Then decide.

What if friends only send you clips?

Tell them once: "I'm off short videos for a month, tell me about it instead." Most will. You can also ask for a screenshot or wait until you see them. If something important happens, it will reach you another way.

What to do if you slip

  1. Stop at the clip you are on. Ten minutes is better than two hours.
  2. Delete the app again right away. Do not leave it for tomorrow.
  3. Restart the streak today. One slip after 12 clean days still means 12 clean days.
  4. Fix the cause. Where were you, what time was it, how did you feel? Change one thing about that moment.
  5. Do not slip twice in a row. That is the rule that saves the month.

After day 30

You have three honest options. Stay off, if your evenings are better without it. Come back with a fence: laptop only, 20 minutes, not after 9 pm, and quit again if you break the fence twice in a week. Or extend to 60 or 90 days and combine it with a bigger goal, such as monk mode or a Winter Arc. If your problem is wider than one feed, try the 7-day dopamine detox plan.

If you keep trying and cannot stop, or scrolling is how you cope with anxiety, low mood or poor sleep, talk to a doctor or therapist. That is a common reason to ask for help, and a good one.

FAQ

How do I quit TikTok for good?

Delete the app, log out everywhere, cut other short-video feeds at the same time and replace your three main trigger moments. Commit to 30 days with a fixed end date, then decide if you want it back.

Is it better to delete TikTok or limit it?

If limits have failed before, a clean break is usually easier because you decide once instead of many times a day. You can come back later with firm limits.

Does deleting the TikTok app delete my account?

No. Removing the app leaves the account in place. You have to deactivate or delete the account separately in the settings.

How long does it take to stop wanting to scroll?

It varies. The first week is usually the hardest, and many people find the automatic reach for the phone fades over the first few weeks.
