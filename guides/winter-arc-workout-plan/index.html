The Winter Arc workout plan

Twelve weeks, four gym days a week, built on the basic lifts. It works for beginners and for people who have trained before, and it tells you exactly when to add weight.

Published 17 November 2026 · 4 min read

How the plan is built

This is an upper/lower split: two upper-body days and two lower-body days a week. Each muscle gets trained twice a week, which is plenty for steady progress, and four days leaves room for work, school and recovery during a Winter Arc. If you can only train three days, rotate the four sessions in order so the week does not matter.

  • Monday: Upper A
  • Tuesday: Lower A
  • Thursday: Upper B
  • Friday: Lower B
  • Wednesday and weekend: rest, walk 8,000+ steps or do light cardio

The workouts

Upper A

  • Bench press: 3 sets of 6-8
  • Barbell or chest-supported row: 3 sets of 8-10
  • Overhead press: 3 sets of 8-10
  • Lat pulldown or pull-ups: 3 sets of 8-12
  • Lateral raise: 2 sets of 12-15
  • Triceps pushdown: 2 sets of 10-12

Lower A

  • Squat: 3 sets of 5-8
  • Romanian deadlift: 3 sets of 8-10
  • Walking lunge or split squat: 2 sets of 10 per leg
  • Leg curl: 2 sets of 10-12
  • Standing calf raise: 3 sets of 10-15
  • Plank: 3 sets of 30-60 seconds

Upper B

  • Incline dumbbell press: 3 sets of 8-10
  • Pull-ups or assisted pull-ups: 3 sets of 6-10
  • Seated cable row: 3 sets of 10-12
  • Dumbbell shoulder press: 2 sets of 10-12
  • Biceps curl: 3 sets of 10-12
  • Face pull: 2 sets of 12-15

Lower B

  • Deadlift or trap-bar deadlift: 3 sets of 4-6
  • Leg press: 3 sets of 10-12
  • Bulgarian split squat: 2 sets of 8-10 per leg
  • Leg extension: 2 sets of 12-15
  • Seated calf raise: 3 sets of 12-15
  • Hanging knee raise: 3 sets of 10-15

How to add weight: double progression

Every exercise has a rep range, like 6-8. Pick a weight you can lift for the bottom of the range with good form. Each session, try to add a rep. When you hit the top of the range on every working set, add the smallest weight jump next time (usually 2.5 kg / 5 lb on big lifts, 1-2 kg / 2.5-5 lb on small ones) and start again at the bottom of the range.

Example: bench press 3 x 6-8 at 60 kg. Week 1: 8, 7, 6. Week 2: 8, 8, 7. Week 3: 8, 8, 8. Week 4: 62.5 kg for 6, 6, 6. That is progressive overload without guessing, and it is the single most important part of this plan.

Every session

  • Write down last time's weight and reps before you start
  • Warm up with 2-3 lighter sets on the first big lift
  • Stop each working set 1-2 reps before failure
  • Log every working set
  • Add weight only when every set hit the top of the range

The 12 weeks at a glance

  • Weeks 1-2: learn the lifts. Start lighter than you think. Perfect form, full range of motion.
  • Weeks 3-8: push. Follow double progression on every exercise. This is where most of the strength comes from.
  • Week 9: lighter week. Same exercises, about 60% of the sets, same weights. Joints and motivation recover.
  • Weeks 10-12: test. Keep progressing, and in the last week work up to a heavy set of 3-5 on your main lifts to see how far you came.

Beginners vs intermediates

Beginners (less than a year of consistent training) will add weight almost every week at first. Stay on the plan as written and focus on form and showing up. If a lift feels unsafe, swap it: goblet squats for squats, trap-bar or Romanian deadlifts for conventional deadlifts, machine press for barbell bench.

Intermediates will progress slower. Add a fourth set on the first exercise of each day, and accept that some weeks you add one rep, not weight. That is still progress.

Food and recovery

Training is the stimulus; food and sleep are where the change happens. Aim for 7-9 hours of sleep and a protein target around 1.6 g per kg of body weight. If you want to lose fat, eat in a modest deficit and expect strength to rise more slowly. If you want to build muscle, eat at maintenance or slightly above. If you have a medical condition or an injury, check with a doctor or physio before starting.

Keep it going after New Year

After week 12, take an easy week and start a new 12-week block with the same structure, swapping one or two exercises for variety. The plan does not expire on 31 December, and neither should the habit. Use the Winter Arc checklist to keep the rest of your day on track.

FAQ

How many days a week should I train during a Winter Arc?

Three to five days works for most people. This plan uses four, which gives each muscle two sessions a week and still leaves time to recover.

Can a beginner follow this Winter Arc workout plan?

Yes. Start with lighter weights, learn the lifts in the first two weeks and swap any exercise that feels unsafe for an easier version, such as goblet squats instead of barbell squats.

When should I increase the weight?

When you reach the top of the rep range on every working set of an exercise, add the smallest weight jump next session and start again at the bottom of the range.

Can I do this plan at home?

With adjustable dumbbells and a bench you can do most of it. Replace machines with dumbbell or bodyweight versions and keep using double progression.
