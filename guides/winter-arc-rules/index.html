Winter Arc rules: the core list and how to pick yours

Nobody owns the Winter Arc, so there is no official rulebook. But the same rules show up again and again, and some of them work far better than others. Here is the full list, what each rule is for, and how to choose a set that survives a bad week.

Published 23 November 2026 · 6 min read

What are the Winter Arc rules?

A Winter Arc is a fixed stretch of time, usually in the last months of the year, when you follow a short list of daily rules to become fitter and more focused. The rules are the arc. Without them it is just a cold season and a playlist.

Most people end up with some version of these seven: train, eat enough protein, sleep on a schedule, do the hard thing first, cut one habit, learn something daily, and track it all. Everything else is a variation. Start from that core, then make it yours.

The full rule list, by area

Body

  • Train 3 to 6 days a week. Choose a number you can hit in a busy week, not your best week.
  • Walk every day. 8,000 steps is a good default, and it covers rest days too.
  • Hit a protein target. About 0.7 g per pound of body weight (1.6 g per kg).
  • Sleep 7 to 9 hours with the same wake-up time every day.
  • Drink mostly water. Keep coffee to the first half of the day if sleep is a problem.

Mind

  • Read 10 pages a day. Paper if possible, so the phone stays away.
  • Write three lines in a journal at night. What went well, what did not, what comes first tomorrow.
  • Learn one skill for 30 minutes. A language, code, an instrument, a certificate.
  • Get outside in daylight. Ten to twenty minutes early in the day helps when mornings are dark.

Focus and work

  • Hardest task before noon. Before email, before chat, before the feed.
  • One block of 60 to 90 minutes without a phone in the room.
  • Plan tomorrow tonight. Three to five tasks, each rated easy, medium, hard or brutal.

Things to cut

  • Short-form video. The most common "don't" for a reason. Here is how to quit it.
  • Alcohol. If you drink daily or feel unwell when you stop, talk to a doctor first. Stopping suddenly can be dangerous if you are dependent.
  • Sugary drinks and late-night snacks.
  • Phone in bed. Charge it in another room.
  • Impulse spending. A 48-hour wait on anything you do not need.

How to pick your 5 rules

Twenty rules look great in a video and collapse in week two. Five is enough to change a season. Use this filter:

  1. Name one outcome. "By the last day I want to ___." Lose 10 pounds, finish a course, stop scrolling at night.
  2. Choose three rules that cause that outcome. For fat loss: a calorie target, protein and daily steps. For studying: a daily block, a fixed wake-up time and no phone before noon.
  3. Add one foundation rule. Sleep is usually the best one. Everything else gets harder when you are tired.
  4. Add one "don't". One clean cut, stated as a yes or no.
  5. Run the bad-day test. Could you keep this rule when you are tired, busy and travelling? If not, shrink it until you could.

Write rules you can check

A good rule can be answered with yes or no at 10 pm. Compare:

  • "Eat healthier" becomes "at least 130 g of protein".
  • "Use my phone less" becomes "no phone until 9 am".
  • "Work on my project" becomes "60 minutes on the project before lunch".
  • "Get fit" becomes "lift Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday".

Three ready-made rule sets

The beginner arc

  • Lift 3 days a week, walk 7,000 steps on the others
  • Protein at every meal
  • Same wake-up time, all week
  • Read 10 pages a day
  • No phone in bed

The lean arc

  • Lift 4 days a week and log every set
  • Hit calories and protein (see the Winter Arc diet)
  • 8,000 steps a day
  • In bed for 8 hours
  • No alcohol and no sugary drinks

The focus arc

  • Two 60-minute deep-work blocks before noon
  • One task rated hard or brutal every day
  • Train 3 days a week, short and simple
  • Three lines of journal at night
  • No short-form video

What happens when you break a rule?

Some challenges send you back to day one after a single miss. A Winter Arc does not have to. Restarting in week seven because of one lost evening teaches you to quit, not to continue. A better set of penalties:

  • Never miss twice. One miss is an accident. The next day is not negotiable.
  • Use a minimum version. Fifteen minutes of training, one page, one glass of water and one task still count as showing up.
  • Restart the streak, not the arc. If you slip on your "don't", that streak goes back to zero. The end date stays where it is.
  • Write down why. Two lines are enough. After three misses you will see the pattern.
  • Change rules only on Sundays. Never in the moment, when you are tired and looking for a way out.

FAQ

What are the official Winter Arc rules?

There are no official rules. The most common ones are to train most days, eat enough protein, sleep on a schedule, do your hardest task first, cut one bad habit, learn daily and track everything.

How many rules should a Winter Arc have?

Five is a good number, and seven is about the limit for most people. Every rule should be something you can answer with yes or no at the end of the day.

What happens if you break a Winter Arc rule?

You keep going the next day and do not miss twice in a row. If you slip on something you cut out, restart that streak but keep your end date.

Can I change my Winter Arc rules halfway through?

Yes, but do it at a planned weekly review and change one thing at a time. Do not drop a rule in the moment because the day feels hard.

Are the Winter Arc rules different for women?

No. The same structure works for everyone. Set calories, protein and training for your own body and goals, and check with a doctor if you are pregnant or have a health condition.
