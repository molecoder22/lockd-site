How to stop doomscrolling and get your evenings back

You wanted to check one headline. Forty minutes later you feel worse and know little more. Doomscrolling is a loop with a trigger, an action and a small reward, and you can break it at each point. Here is how, with a phone setup and a 14-day plan.

Published 25 November 2026 · 6 min read

What is doomscrolling?

Doomscrolling is scrolling through bad or upsetting news and posts for longer than you meant to, even though it makes you feel worse. It is close to general endless scrolling, but the pull is different. You are looking for certainty. Something worrying happened, your mind wants to know if you are safe, and the next post might tell you. It rarely does, so you read another one.

Staying informed is fine. The line is simple: if you stop learning anything new and keep scrolling, it has become doomscrolling.

Why it is so hard to stop

  • Feeds have no end. There is no last page that tells you to stop.
  • Threats grab attention. Our minds notice danger first, and alarming posts get more reactions, so you see more of them.
  • It feels useful. Reading about a problem feels like doing something about it.
  • It fills a tired moment. Most doomscrolling happens when you are too worn out to choose something else.

None of that is a character flaw. It does mean that "I'll just stop" is a weak plan. Change the setup first and the decision gets easier.

Step 1: find your trigger

For two days, change nothing. Each time you catch yourself scrolling, write down three things: the time, where you are and what happened right before. You will probably see one or two of these patterns:

  • The bed trigger: phone in hand, lights off, "just five minutes".
  • The gap trigger: waiting, commercials, the minute between two tasks.
  • The stress trigger: a hard task, an argument, a worrying message.
  • The alert trigger: a breaking-news notification pulls you in.
  • The wake-up trigger: the phone is the alarm, so the feed is the first thing you see.

Your plan only has to solve your top two.

Step 2: set up your phone in 15 minutes

  1. Turn off news and social notifications. Keep calls, messages from people and true emergency alerts.
  2. Delete the worst app or log out so that you have to type the password each time.
  3. Clear your first home screen. Leave only tools: calendar, maps, camera, notes.
  4. Set an app limit. On iPhone, Settings, then Screen Time, then App Limits. Limits are easy to tap past, so treat them as a reminder and not as a wall.
  5. Move the charger out of the bedroom and buy a simple alarm clock.
  6. Try grayscale after 8 pm. Some people find a gray feed much easier to leave.

If short videos are your main problem, removing them completely tends to be easier than limiting them. Our guide on how to quit short videos goes through it step by step.

Step 3: give the news a place

You do not have to choose between panic and ignorance. Set two fixed windows of 10 to 15 minutes, for example at noon and at 5 pm. Use one or two sources that have an end: a daily newsletter, a printed paper, a short news podcast. When you finish, you are done until the next window. Nothing after dinner.

If a story really worries you, ask one question: is there something I can do about it today? If yes, do it: vote, donate, call, prepare. If no, close it.

Step 4: replace the scroll, by trigger

  • In bed: a paperback and a lamp. If your mind is racing, write the worries down and the first step for each.
  • In gaps: look up, stretch, drink water, or let yourself be bored for a minute.
  • Under stress: stand up, ten slow breaths, then two minutes on the task you are avoiding.
  • After waking: daylight, water and a shower before any screen.
  • Tired in the evening: a walk, a call, music, or simply bed.

Have the replacement in reach before you need it. The book has to lie on the pillow, not on the shelf.

Your anti-doomscroll setup

  • My two main triggers written down
  • News and social notifications off
  • Worst app deleted or logged out
  • Charger out of the bedroom
  • Two news windows set, none after dinner
  • One replacement ready for each trigger
  • A daily check-in at 5 pm, before the hard hours

How to stop in the middle of a scroll

You will still end up in the feed now and then. Use this when you notice it:

  1. Name it. Say "I'm doomscrolling" out loud.
  2. Put the phone face down and stand up.
  3. Move to another room without it.
  4. Do one physical thing for two minutes: dishes, push-ups, stairs.
  5. Then decide whether you want to go back. Often you will not.

A 14-day plan to stop doomscrolling

Days 1-2: watch

Write down each scroll session and your screen time. No changes yet.

Days 3-5: set up

Do the 15-minute phone setup. Charger out of the bedroom from day three, without exceptions.

Days 6-9: windows only

News only in your two windows. Expect to feel as if you are missing something. Note at the end of each day what you actually missed. Usually it is nothing that changed your day.

Days 10-14: use the time

Put one thing you want into the freed hour: training, reading, a project, people. If you take back 45 minutes a day, that is more than five hours a week. On day 14, compare your screen time with day one and decide what stays.

When it is more than a habit

If the scrolling goes along with constant worry, low mood, panic or weeks of bad sleep, speak with a doctor or therapist. Doomscrolling can feed anxiety, and anxiety can drive the scrolling, so it helps to treat both. If you feel unsafe or have thoughts of harming yourself, in the US and Canada you can call or text 988 at any time.

FAQ

How do I stop doomscrolling at night?

Charge the phone outside the bedroom, use a separate alarm clock and put a book on your pillow. Set your last news check before dinner, so there is nothing left to look up in bed.

Why can't I stop doomscrolling?

Feeds never end, alarming posts grab attention and reading feels like gaining control. Changing your phone setup and your surroundings usually works better than willpower alone.

Is doomscrolling bad for you?

It often leaves people more stressed and cuts into sleep, focus and time with others. If it affects your mood or sleep for weeks, talk to a doctor or therapist.

How can I stay informed without doomscrolling?

Check the news in two fixed windows of 10 to 15 minutes a day, using sources that have an end, such as a newsletter or a short podcast. Turn off breaking-news alerts.

How long does it take to break the habit?

It varies. Many people notice the automatic reach for the phone getting weaker within a few weeks if the trigger is removed. Start with 14 days and extend to 30.
