Winter Arc

The Winter Arc checklist

A Winter Arc lives or dies on what you do each day. This checklist covers the setup before day one, the daily boxes to tick, a weekly review and what to do on the days you don't feel like it.

Published 17 November 2026 · 4 min read

Before day one: the setup checklist

Most arcs fail in the first week because the decisions were never made. Spend one evening on these and the next 90 days get much easier. If you are new to the idea, read what the Winter Arc is first.

Setup

  • Pick a start date and an end date and write both down
  • Write one sentence about who you want to be on the last day
  • Choose 5 to 7 daily rules you can keep on a bad day
  • Choose one thing you are cutting out completely
  • Set a fixed wake-up time, weekends included
  • Plan your training days for the week
  • Work out a daily protein target (start around 1.6 g per kg of body weight)
  • Take a starting photo and note your weight and main lifts
  • Pick one place to track everything

The last box matters more than it looks. If your tasks are in one app, your workouts in another and your food in a third, you will stop checking at least one of them by week three. Put everything in one place.

The daily Winter Arc checklist

Keep the daily list short. Every box should be something you either did or did not do, with no room for "sort of". Here is a strong default you can adjust:

Every day

  • Up at the planned time, no phone for the first 30 minutes
  • Hardest task of the day done before noon
  • Training session done (or planned rest day)
  • Protein target hit
  • Water: 2 to 3 litres
  • 10 pages read or 30 minutes of study
  • Stayed clean from the thing you cut out
  • Tomorrow's tasks written down before bed
  • In bed on time

Rate the tasks, not just the habits

Not every box is equal. Finishing a hard project is worth more than drinking water, and treating them the same makes it easy to fill a day with easy wins. Give each task a difficulty (easy, medium, hard, brutal) and aim to finish at least one hard one every day.

The weekly checklist

Once a week, usually Sunday evening, take fifteen minutes to look back and plan ahead. This is where you catch problems before they turn into a lost month.

Every Sunday

  • Count the days you hit every rule this week
  • Compare this week's lifts to last week's
  • Check the weight trend, not a single morning
  • Name the one thing that went wrong most often
  • Change one thing for next week (not five)
  • Plan training days and the hardest tasks of the week
  • Prep food or plan meals for the busiest days

The bad-day checklist

You will have bad days: sick, tired, travelling, a family dinner, a deadline. The arc is not ruined by a bad day. It is ruined by turning one bad day into a bad week. On those days, switch to the minimum version:

  • Train for 15 minutes instead of skipping. A walk counts if you are sick.
  • Hit protein even if the rest of the food is not perfect.
  • Keep your one "don't". This is the rule that never bends.
  • Do one task from your list, even a small one.
  • Never miss twice. One missed day is noise. Two in a row is the start of a new habit.

How to know the checklist is working

After two weeks you should notice three things: the morning routine runs without thinking, your training numbers are creeping up, and you feel strange on the days you skip a box. If none of that is happening, your list is probably too long or too vague. Cut it down and make every box a clear yes or no.

The simplest test is to compare each day with the one before it. Not with a fitness influencer, not with your best week ever. Just yesterday. If today was a little better more often than not, the arc is working.

Printable version

Prefer paper? Copy the daily list onto an index card and keep it next to your bed. Tick the boxes before you sleep, and write tomorrow's hardest task at the bottom. Paper works as long as you check it every night; most people switch to their phone because it is always with them. For the training part, use the Winter Arc workout plan.

FAQ

How many items should a Winter Arc checklist have?

Five to nine daily boxes is the sweet spot. Fewer and it is not a challenge; more and you will start skipping the hard ones and ticking only the easy ones.

What should be on a Winter Arc daily checklist?

Wake-up time, one hard task before noon, training, protein, reading or study, staying clean from the habit you cut, planning tomorrow and bedtime. Adjust it to your goals.

What if I miss a day of my Winter Arc?

Do the minimum version the next day and do not miss twice in a row. One missed day does not end the arc; quitting does.

Should I use an app or paper for my checklist?

Either works if you check it every night. An app helps when you also want to track training, food and streaks in the same place and compare each day with the one before.
