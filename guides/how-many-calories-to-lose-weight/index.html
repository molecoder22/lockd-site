How many calories should you eat to lose weight?

There is no single number for everyone, but there is a simple way to find yours. Estimate what you burn, eat a bit less than that, then adjust based on what the scale trend actually does. Here is the method with worked examples.

Published 26 November 2026 · 5 min read

Step 1: estimate your maintenance calories

Maintenance is the number of calories that keeps your weight stable. A widely used way to estimate it is the Mifflin-St Jeor equation for your resting burn, multiplied by an activity factor.

  • Men: 10 x weight in kg + 6.25 x height in cm - 5 x age + 5
  • Women: 10 x weight in kg + 6.25 x height in cm - 5 x age - 161

Then multiply by your activity level:

  • 1.2: desk job, little exercise
  • 1.375: light exercise 1-3 days a week
  • 1.55: moderate exercise 3-5 days a week
  • 1.725: hard training 6-7 days a week or a physical job

In pounds and inches, convert first: kg = lb / 2.2, cm = inches x 2.54. Most people overestimate their activity, so if you are between two levels, pick the lower one.

Step 2: choose your deficit

A deficit of about 300-500 calories a day below maintenance is a good starting point for most people. A rough rule of thumb is that a 500-calorie daily deficit leads to about 1 lb (0.45 kg) of loss per week, though real results vary and slow down over time.

A sensible pace is around 0.5-1% of your body weight per week. Faster than that usually means more hunger, worse training, more muscle loss and a higher chance of quitting. People with more weight to lose can often go toward the faster end at first; leaner people should stay at the slower end.

Common guidance is not to go below about 1,200 calories a day for women or 1,500 for men without medical supervision. If you have a medical condition, are pregnant or breastfeeding, take medication that affects appetite or blood sugar, or have a history of eating disorders, talk to a doctor before you start a diet.

Worked examples

Example 1: a 30-year-old man, 90 kg (198 lb), 180 cm (5'11"), trains 4 days a week

Resting: 900 + 1,125 - 150 + 5 = 1,880. Times 1.55 = about 2,910 maintenance. With a 500-calorie deficit, his starting target is about 2,400 calories a day. Expected pace: up to around 0.5-0.9 kg (1-2 lb) per week.

Example 2: a 35-year-old woman, 75 kg (165 lb), 165 cm (5'5"), lightly active

Resting: 750 + 1,031 - 175 - 161 = about 1,445. Times 1.375 = about 1,990 maintenance. With a 400-calorie deficit, her starting target is about 1,600 calories a day. Expected pace: up to around 0.4-0.7 kg (about 1-1.5 lb) per week.

These are starting points, not promises. The equation can be off by a few hundred calories for any one person. That is why step 3 matters most.

Set your calorie target

  • Calculate maintenance with the equation above
  • Pick the lower activity level if unsure
  • Subtract 300-500 calories
  • Set protein at around 1.6 g per kg of body weight
  • Weigh yourself every morning, same conditions
  • Review the weekly average after 2-3 weeks

Step 3: adjust using your weight trend

Daily weight jumps up and down by a kilo or more because of water, salt, carbs and digestion. Ignore single days. Weigh yourself each morning after the bathroom and before eating, and compare weekly averages.

  • Losing within your target pace: change nothing.
  • No change for 2-3 weeks: first check your logging is honest (oils, drinks, weekends). If it is, reduce by 100-200 calories or add 2,000-3,000 daily steps.
  • Losing faster than about 1% a week for several weeks: add 100-200 calories, especially if you feel weak or your training is suffering.

As you lose weight, your maintenance drops a little too. Expect to recalculate or adjust every 4-6 weeks.

What to eat inside your calories

Calories decide weight loss; what you eat decides how easy it feels. Three things make the biggest difference:

  • Protein at every meal. It keeps you full and helps you keep muscle. Around 1.6 g per kg of body weight a day is a good target.
  • Lots of vegetables and fruit. High volume, few calories.
  • Planned treats instead of banned foods. Fit them into the number rather than swinging between strict and off the rails.

How to track calories without hating it

You do not need to weigh every grain of rice forever. Track carefully for the first few weeks to learn what portions look like, then you can get looser. Repeat the same breakfasts and lunches, which makes logging fast. Estimates from photos are useful for speed but are estimates, so be extra careful with oils, sauces and drinks.

FAQ

How many calories should I eat to lose weight?

Estimate your maintenance calories and eat about 300-500 below that. Then adjust every 2-3 weeks based on your weekly average weight.

Is 1,200 calories a day enough to lose weight?

It is a common lower limit for women and too low for many people, especially if they are active. Going below it should only happen under medical supervision.

Why am I not losing weight in a calorie deficit?

Usually the deficit is smaller than you think because of untracked oils, drinks or weekends, or water retention is hiding fat loss. Track carefully and judge by 2-3 weeks of averages.

How fast can I safely lose weight?

Around 0.5-1% of your body weight per week is a sensible pace for most adults. Check with a doctor if you have a medical condition.
