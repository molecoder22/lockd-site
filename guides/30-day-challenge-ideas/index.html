Challenges

30 day challenge ideas that are worth doing

Thirty days is the easiest length to commit to: long enough to notice a change, short enough to finish. Below are 30 ideas sorted by area, each with a clear rule you can check every day, followed by a simple way to choose one and see it through.

Published 26 November 2026 · 4 min read

What makes a good 30 day challenge

A good challenge has one rule that you can answer yes or no each night. "Eat healthier" fails that test. "Protein at every meal" passes. It should also be a little uncomfortable but possible on your worst day, and it should leave you with something you want to keep after day 30.

Pick one challenge, or two at most. Five at once feels exciting on day 1 and falls apart by day 9.

Body: 8 ideas

  • 8,000 steps a day. Walk after meals if you are short.
  • Train 3-4 days a week on a written plan. Same days every week. Log every set.
  • 100 push-ups a day, split into as many sets as you need. Scale to knee push-ups if you must.
  • 10 minutes of mobility every morning: hips, shoulders and upper back.
  • Protein at every meal, aiming for around 1.6 g per kg of body weight per day in total.
  • No sugary drinks. Water, coffee, tea and zero-calorie drinks only.
  • Cook dinner at home 5 nights a week.
  • Same bedtime every night, within 30 minutes, for 7-9 hours of sleep.

Mind and focus: 7 ideas

  • Read 10 pages a day. About two short books in a month.
  • Journal three lines before bed: what went well, what did not, what is first tomorrow.
  • One 60-minute deep-work block a day, phone in another room.
  • Learn one skill for 20 minutes a day: a language, an instrument, coding, drawing.
  • Hardest task first. Rate tomorrow's tasks the night before and start with the hardest one.
  • 10 minutes of quiet with no input: a walk with no podcast counts.
  • One cold shower finish a day, 30-60 seconds. Skip it if you have a heart condition.

Phone and dopamine: 6 ideas

  • No phone for the first 30 minutes after waking. Charge it outside the bedroom.
  • No short-video apps for 30 days. Delete them, do not just mute them.
  • Social media only on a computer, never on the phone.
  • Screens off an hour before bed.
  • Grayscale screen for 30 days. It makes the phone less appealing.
  • No phone at meals, alone or with people.

Money: 4 ideas

  • No spending on takeout or delivery.
  • Write down every purchase the same day, with the amount.
  • 24-hour rule: wait a day before any non-essential purchase.
  • Cancel one subscription a week you would not pay for again today.

Home and life: 5 ideas

  • Make your bed every morning.
  • 10-minute reset every evening: dishes, surfaces, tomorrow's clothes.
  • Throw out or donate one item a day.
  • Message one friend or family member a day, with a real message.
  • Plan tomorrow tonight in under five minutes.

How to pick the right one

Ask which single change would make the most other things easier. For a lot of people that is sleep or the phone, because both affect energy and focus all day. If you already sleep well and do not scroll much, pick training or food. Then write the rule as one sentence and stick it where you will see it.

Set up your 30 days

  • One challenge, written as a yes/no rule
  • Start date and end date on the calendar
  • A minimum version for bad days
  • One obstacle removed before day 1
  • A place to check it off every night
  • A day 30 review already planned

How to make it to day 30

Start small. The rule should feel almost too easy in week one. Never miss twice. One miss is a slip; do the minimum the next day and carry on. Remove friction before you start. If the challenge is no snacks at night, do not buy them. If it is training, pack the bag the night before.

Days 10 to 20 are usually where people quit, because the novelty is gone and the results are small. Expect it. That middle stretch is the actual challenge.

What to do after day 30

Review honestly: what share of days did you keep, and do you want to keep the habit? If yes, keep it as your new normal and add a second challenge on top. If you want something bigger, stack three of these into a 90 day challenge.

FAQ

What is a good 30 day challenge for beginners?

Pick one rule you can keep on a bad day, such as 8,000 steps, reading 10 pages, or no phone for the first 30 minutes after waking.

Can I do more than one 30 day challenge at once?

You can do two if they are small. More than that usually falls apart in the second week, so add more after you finish the first one.

What if I miss a day of my 30 day challenge?

Do not start over. Do the minimum version the next day and keep going. Never miss two days in a row.

What are good 30 day challenges for self-improvement?

Reading daily, a morning without the phone, training on a written plan, journaling before bed and a fixed bedtime all pay off well beyond the 30 days.
