Challenge

A flexible 75-day challenge that survives bad days

Strict challenges work well for some people. Others get sick on day 19, miss one rule and never start again. If you want a 75 Hard alternative with the same seriousness and a better answer to real life, here is a full plan: the rules, the minimums, the miss rule and the checkpoints.

Published 25 November 2026 · 6 min read

Why look for a 75 Hard alternative?

75 Hard, created by Andy Frisella, is a fixed program. In short: two 45-minute workouts a day with one outdoors, a diet with no alcohol or cheat meals, a gallon of water, 10 pages of nonfiction and a daily progress photo. Miss anything and you go back to day one. The strictness is the point, and many people like exactly that.

It is not the right tool for everyone. You may want something else if:

  • You work shifts, have kids or travel, and two sessions a day will not fit.
  • You are new to training and need rest days to recover.
  • You know that going back to zero makes you quit instead of making you careful.
  • Your goal is a project or an exam, and a fitness program only covers part of it.

Softer versions exist too, and their rules differ depending on who wrote them. The plan below is our own. It is flexible about how much you do and strict about whether you show up.

The flexible 75-day challenge: five rules

  1. Move every day. Four training days and three easy days a week. An easy day is a 30-minute walk or stretching.
  2. Eat to one clear target. Protein is the simplest: around 1.6 g per kg of body weight (0.7 g per pound). Add a calorie target if your goal is to lose or gain weight.
  3. Do one hard thing before noon. Study, the project, the phone call you keep moving to tomorrow.
  4. Cut one thing completely. Short videos, alcohol, sugary drinks, gaming on weeknights. One clear line.
  5. Read 10 pages and write three lines. What went well, what did not, what is first tomorrow.

Five is enough. If you want to swap one, use our list of Winter Arc rules and keep the total at five.

Set a full version and a minimum version

This is the part that keeps you going for 75 days. Before day one, write two versions of every rule. The full version is what you do on a normal day. The minimum is what you do when you are tired, traveling or buried in work. Both count as done.

  • Training: full is 45 to 60 minutes. Minimum is 15 minutes: squats, push-ups, rows or a brisk walk.
  • Food: full is your protein and calorie target. Minimum is a palm-sized protein portion at every meal.
  • Hard thing: full is 90 minutes. Minimum is 20 minutes on the first step.
  • Cut: no minimum here. It is yes or no, which makes it easier to keep.
  • Read and write: full is 10 pages and three lines. Minimum is two pages and one line.

Put a limit on it: at most two minimum days a week. If you need more than that, the full version is too big for your life right now. Shrink it once, on purpose, and carry on.

Write this down before day one

  • Start date and end date, 75 days apart
  • Five rules, each with a full and a minimum version
  • The one thing I am cutting out
  • My training days and easy days
  • Starting weight, waist and one photo, if those matter for my goal
  • Dates of the three checkpoints: day 25, day 50 and day 75
  • My miss rule

What happens when you miss a day?

Decide now, while you are calm. A good miss rule costs something and does not cost everything:

  1. A miss is a miss. Write it down.
  2. Never miss the same rule twice in a row. That is the line you defend.
  3. Every missed day adds one day to the end. Miss three days and you finish on day 78.
  4. Three misses in one week trigger a review. Sit down for 15 minutes and change the rule or the schedule.

Adding days keeps you honest because there is a price. Your 40 good days still count, so there is no reason to drop the whole thing on day 41.

The 75 days in three blocks

Days 1-25: show up

Keep everything easy enough to finish. Train with weights you control and stop with two reps left in the tank. Learn five meals you can make without thinking. The only score that matters in this block is how many days you completed.

Days 26-50: push one thing

Pick one rule and raise it. Add weight in the gym when you hit the top of your rep range on every set, or stretch the hard block from 90 minutes to two hours. Raise one thing, not five. Motivation is usually gone by now, so this is where a routine pays off. See how to be disciplined.

Days 51-75: make it yours

Ask which rules you want to keep on day 76 and practice them the way you will really live them.

The three checkpoints

On days 25, 50 and 75, spend 20 minutes on the same questions:

  • How many days did I complete, out of how many?
  • How many were minimum days?
  • What improved that I can measure: weight trend, lifts, hours worked, clean days?
  • Which rule do I dodge most, and what is the real reason?
  • What do I change for the next 25 days?

Stay safe

Take at least one real rest day a week if you are new to lifting. Drink to thirst and more when you sweat; you do not need to force a fixed amount of water. Stop if you feel sharp pain, dizziness or chest pain and get it checked. If you have a medical condition, are pregnant or have a history of disordered eating, talk to a doctor before you start and skip the calorie target and photos unless they agree.

FAQ

What is a good alternative to 75 Hard?

A 75-day challenge with five daily rules, a full and a minimum version of each, and a rule that a missed day adds a day to the end. It stays serious but one bad day does not send you back to zero.

Do I have to restart if I miss a day?

Not in this plan. You record the miss, never miss the same rule twice in a row, and add one day to the end. If you follow a fixed program with its own restart rule, that program's rules apply.

How many workouts a week should a 75-day challenge have?

Four training days plus three easy days with a walk or stretching works for most healthy adults. Beginners can start with three.

Can I do a 75-day challenge for something other than fitness?

Yes. Keep one movement rule and one food rule for energy, and point the rest at your exam, project or the habit you want to quit.

What should I do after 75 days?

Keep the two or three rules that changed the most, drop the rest, take an easy week and then pick your next goal with a new end date.
